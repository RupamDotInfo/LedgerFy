# Payroll compliance sources

**Status:** the rules below were verified against primary or official sources
before they were implemented. This document is the evidence register for
`docs/payroll-accounting-policy.md`. It records **what** each rule is, **where
it came from**, **from when it applies**, and — importantly — **how confident
we are**, so that a rule we cannot establish is blocked rather than guessed.

**Verified on:** 21 October 2026
**Rule:** no obligation is implemented on the strength of a blog post. Where
sources conflict or a rule is still in draft, the obligation is implemented as
*not in force* and the final payroll run is refused until a person with the
authority to decide acknowledges it.

---

## 1. How to read this register

| Status | Meaning | What the application does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `In force` | Confirmed by an official notification or an official department page | Calculated automatically |
| `In force, state-dependent` | The central rule is in force; the state rules that make it enforceable vary | Calculated against the company's chosen jurisdiction, with the state named in the review |
| `Proposed` | Published in draft or as a proposed schedule; not established as in force | **Not** applied automatically. The rule set is offered, the payroll run is refused, and a person must acknowledge it |
| `Out of scope` | Deliberately not implemented (see `docs/payroll-accounting-policy.md` §9) | Not calculated at all |

## 2. Employees' Provident Fund (EPF)

**Source:** Employees' Provident Funds & Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952,
as replaced by Chapter III of the **Code on Social Security, 2020**; the
**Employees' Provident Fund Scheme, 2026**, **Employees' Pension Scheme, 2026**
and **Employees' Deposit Linked Insurance Scheme, 2026** notified on
**29 June 2026**; and the Ministry of Labour & Employment **Notification
S.O. 5109(E) dated 17 September 2026**.

| Item | Rule | From | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Employee contribution | 12% of basic wages + dearness allowance (and retaining allowance, where paid) | In force | `In force` |
| Employer contribution | 12% of the same wages | In force | `In force` |
| Employer's split | 8.33% to the Pension Scheme, 3.67% to the Provident Fund | In force | `In force` |
| Wage ceiling | ₹15,000 per month, **raised to ₹25,000 per month** | **17 September 2026** (S.O. 5109(E)), superseding S.O. 2702(E) of 29 May 2026 | `In force` |
| Pension ceiling | The 8.33% diversion is now made on wages up to ₹25,000 (₹2,083 a month) | 17 September 2026 | `In force` |
| Government's EPS contribution | 1.16% of wages, still limited to ₹15,000 a month | unchanged | `In force` |
| Reduced rate | 10% for establishments with fewer than 20 employees or as notified | 1 May 2020 | `In force` |
| Voluntary contribution | Above the statutory rate, with the scheme's conditions | In force | `In force` |

**The month of the change is pro-rated.** The rules effective from 17 September
2026 apply to wages for that month by the number of days on each side of the
change, and the employer's share is split 3.67% / 8.33% on each part. The
application therefore supports a **wage ceiling that changes part-way through a
month**, because the notification says so.

**Coverage.** Mandatory for an establishment covered by the Act with employees
drawing up to the ceiling; a higher earner may be a member on the scheme's
conditions. The application asks for the establishment's coverage status rather
than guessing it.

## 3. Employees' State Insurance (ESIC)

**Source:** ESI (General) Regulations, 1950 as amended — the employee rate
fixed by **G.S.R. 423(E) dated 13 June 2019** (effective 1 July 2019) and the
wage ceiling fixed by **G.S.R. 1166(E) dated 22 December 2016** (effective
1 January 2017); the **Employees' State Insurance (General) Regulations 1950**;
and the ESI Corporation's own contribution page, **esic.gov.in/contribution**.

| Item | Rule | From | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Employee contribution | 0.75% of wages | 1 July 2019 | `In force` |
| Employer contribution | 3.25% of wages | 1 July 2019 | `In force` |
| Total | 4% of wages | 1 July 2019 | `In force` |
| Wage ceiling | ₹21,000 per month | 1 January 2017 | `In force` |
| Ceiling for persons with disability | ₹25,000 per month | In force | `In force` |
| Daily-wage exemption | An employee whose average daily wage is up to ₹176 pays no employee share; the employer still pays 3.25% | In force | `In force` |
| Establishment threshold | Factories with 10 or more employees; other establishments by notification | In force | `In force` |
| Contribution period | 1 April – 30 September and 1 October – 31 March; once covered in a period, the coverage continues to the end of that period | In force | `In force` |
| Remittance | By the 15th of the following month | In force | `In force` |
| Area of operation | Pan-India since the Code on Social Security came into force (21 November 2025); the old "notified areas" limit no longer applies | 21 November 2025 | `In force` |

**The wage base.** From 21 November 2025 "wages" follow the Code on Social
Security: basic wages plus dearness allowance (and retaining allowance where
paid). HRA, conveyance and similar allowances are excluded, **unless the
excluded items exceed half of the total remuneration, in which case the excess
is added back**. The application therefore needs the employee's allowance
structure to compute the ESI wage base, and the 50% add-back is part of the
rule, not an option.

**No revision to the ceiling had been notified as at the date of this
register.** A change would be a new effective-dated rule version, and a
payroll run dated after it would pick it up automatically.

## 4. Professional tax — West Bengal

**Source:** the **West Bengal State Tax on Professions, Trades, Callings and
Employments Act, 1979**, and the Finance Department schedule for salaried
employees.

### 4.1 The schedule in force

| Monthly gross wages | Professional tax a month | A year |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up to ₹10,000 | Nil | Nil |
| ₹10,001 – ₹15,000 | ₹110 | ₹1,320 |
| ₹15,001 – ₹25,000 | ₹130 | ₹1,560 |
| ₹25,001 – ₹40,000 | ₹150 | ₹1,800 |
| Above ₹40,000 | ₹200 | ₹2,400 |

Maximum ₹2,400 a year for a salaried employee, within the ₹2,500 ceiling of
Article 276 of the Constitution. Deduction is monthly. A professional tax
deduction is allowed as a deduction from salary income.

**Status: `In force`.**

### 4.2 A replacement schedule, proposed from 1 October 2026

| Monthly gross wages | Professional tax a month | A year |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up to ₹20,000 | Nil | Nil |
| ₹20,001 – ₹30,000 | ₹100 | ₹1,200 |
| ₹30,001 – ₹50,000 | ₹140 | ₹1,680 |
| ₹50,001 – ₹1,00,000 | ₹170 | ₹2,040 |
| Above ₹1,00,000 | ₹208 | ₹2,496 |

**Status: `Proposed` — and this is a deliberate restriction.**

The schedule was published for objections as Notification No. 1407-F.T. of
**18 August 2026** in the Kolkata Gazette Extraordinary. Secondary sources
conflict on its status: some report it as notified and effective from
1 October 2026, and at least one tracks it as a draft with no final
notification found. **We could not establish it as in force from an official
notification**, so:

- the schedule is **not** applied automatically;
- the payroll run for a period on or after 1 October 2026 is **refused** with
  the reason stated, and
- a person with the authority to decide may acknowledge the schedule
  explicitly, which records the acknowledgement in the audit trail.

That is the difference between a payroll system that is right and one that is
confidently wrong. The alternative — silently applying a draft schedule —
would change every employee's take-home pay in the state.

### 4.3 Other states

Professional tax is a state levy and the slabs differ. The application
therefore models it as a **per-jurisdiction, effective-dated rule set**. Only
West Bengal is shipped, because it is the only schedule we could verify. A
company in another state gets no professional tax until that state's schedule
is entered and acknowledged by the company — and the application says so,
rather than applying West Bengal's slabs.

## 5. Salary tax deducted at source

**Source:** the Income Tax Department's **TDS Compliance FAQs**
(incometax.gov.in) and the department's **TDS Rates** table for the tax year
2026-27, and **Section 192 of the Income-tax Act, 1961**, as replaced by
**Section 392 of the Income-tax Act, 2025**.

### 5.1 The change of Act

> "The Act governing TDS depends on when the 'earlier of the event of credit
> or payment' occurs. If the earlier event occurs on or before 31st March,
> 2026, the Income tax Act, 1961 will be applicable. However, if the earlier
> event occurs on or after 1st April 2026, the provisions of the Income Tax
> Act, 2025 shall be applicable."

> "No. The TDS rates and monetary thresholds for all categories of payments
> have been retained as they are under the Income Tax Act, 1961."

**The rates did not change; the section numbers and the year boundaries
did.** The application therefore selects the tax year by the **date of
payment**, and the employer must reset the computation on 1 April.

### 5.2 The slabs

The default regime is the new regime (Section 115BAC of the 1961 Act;
Section 202 of the 2025 Act).

| Taxable income | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Up to ₹4,00,000 | Nil |
| ₹4,00,001 – ₹8,00,000 | 5% above ₹4,00,000 |
| ₹8,00,001 – ₹12,00,000 | ₹20,000 + 10% above ₹8,00,000 |
| ₹12,00,001 – ₹16,00,000 | ₹60,000 + 15% above ₹12,00,000 |
| ₹16,00,001 – ₹20,00,000 | ₹1,00,000 + 20% above ₹16,00,000 |
| ₹20,00,001 – ₹24,00,000 | ₹1,40,000 + 25% above ₹20,00,000 |
| Above ₹24,00,000 | ₹1,80,000 + 30% above ₹24,00,000 |

| Item | Rule | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic exemption, new regime | ₹4,00,000 | `In force` |
| Standard deduction, new regime | ₹75,000 | `In force` |
| Rebate | No tax where the total income is up to ₹12,00,000 (Section 87A) | `In force` |
| Health and education cess | 4% on the income tax plus surcharge | `In force` |
| Surcharge | On income above ₹50,00,000 | `In force` |
| No tax | Where the net taxable income is below ₹4,00,000 (new regime) or ₹2,50,000 (old regime, other than a senior citizen) | `In force` |
| Form | Form 16 becomes **Form 130** from tax year 2026-27 | `In force` |
| Deposit | By the 7th of the following month; March deductions by 30 April | `In force` |
| Return | Quarterly, on Form 24Q for salary | `In force` |

**The old regime** is implemented as an option with its own slabs and its own
exemption limits, and the employee's declared regime is recorded with the run
so that a later change never rewrites history.

**What is not implemented:** the application does not file, deposit or
acknowledge any tax. It computes the deduction and produces the figures for a
return; the filing itself is a government portal, and no filing status is ever
shown because none has been received.

## 6. The labour codes

**Source:** PIB press release **PRID 2192463** of 21 November 2025 (the four
Codes in force), the Ministry of Labour & Employment's **"What's New"** page
and **"Labour Codes"** page, and the departmental notifications of **8 May 2026**
(Central Rules under the Code on Wages, the Industrial Relations Code and the
Code on Social Security) and **9 May 2026** (the OSH Code).

| Item | Rule | From | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The four Codes | Code on Wages 2019, Industrial Relations Code 2020, Code on Social Security 2020, OSHWC Code 2020 — in force, replacing 29 laws | 21 November 2025 | `In force` |
| Central Rules | Notified under all four Codes | 8–9 May 2026 | `In force` |
| Social security schemes | The EPF Scheme 2026, EPS Scheme 2026 and EDLI Scheme 2026 notified | 29 June 2026 | `In force` |
| State rules | Each state notifies its own; until it does, the earlier laws continue to apply there. As at 20 October 2026, six of thirty-seven states and union territories had notified rules for all four Codes | — | `In force, state-dependent` |

**Why this matters for payroll.** The EPF and ESIC figures above are computed
under the Code on Social Security. The application records the jurisdiction it
computed for and shows it in the payroll review, because the same employee is
governed by different rules in different states.

## 7. What is deliberately not implemented

| Item | Why | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Statutory bonus | The minimum-wage floor is verifiable, but the full calculation is a separate obligation with its own edge cases. The threshold (₹21,000 a month, computed on the applicable minimum wage or ₹7,000, notified on 25 August 2026 with retrospective effect from 21 November 2025) is recorded here for reference only | `Out of scope` |
| Gratuity | The eligibility and the calculation have several interacting conditions | `Out of scope` |
| Leave encashment | Not a calculation this system performs | `Out of scope` |
| Equal remuneration | A governance obligation, not a payroll calculation | `Out of scope` |
| Any filing, deposit or challan | Requires a government portal and a credential this application does not have | `Out of scope` |

## 8. The rules this application ships

Every rule the calculation uses is an **effective-dated row** with a source, a
status and an acknowledgement flag. The application holds these:

| Rule set | Versions shipped |
| --- | --- |
| EPF | Ceiling ₹15,000 up to 16 September 2026; ceiling ₹25,000 from 17 September 2026, with the part-month split. The reduced 10% rate as an explicit option. |
| ESIC | Ceiling ₹21,000 (₹25,000 for persons with disability), 0.75% / 3.25%, the ₹176 daily-wage exemption, the Code wage base with the 50% add-back. |
| Professional tax | West Bengal, the schedule in force. The schedule proposed from 1 October 2026 is held as `Proposed` and requires an acknowledgement. |
| Salary TDS | Section 192 for payments on or before 31 March 2026; Section 392 for payments on or after 1 April 2026. New-regime and old-regime slabs, the rebate, the cess and the surcharge. |

A payroll run states, for every employee, which version of each rule it used.
That statement is stored in the run's snapshot, so a run can be explained years
later, and a later change to a rule never rewrites it.
